Abstract

Videos are widely used to communicate expert knowledge across specialized domains. This knowledge is often conveyed through visual knowledge anchors, which are temporally localized instructional units that pair informative visuals with their spoken explanations. Existing methods collect these anchors through fixed pipelines without understanding the visual content. As a result, they need additional semantic post-processing and domain-specific tuning. GUI agents offer a promising alternative, as they use VLMs to understand visual content and interact with video players through the same interfaces people use. Most existing video agents answer predefined questions or complete downstream tasks. Their evaluations focus on final results and reveal little about how they collect visual knowledge anchors from educational videos. To address this gap, we propose LEAF (Lecture Explorer for Anchor Finding), an agent harness for collecting visual knowledge anchors through interactive video browsing. It skims the video, pauses at informative visuals, and rewinds to find where they begin. It then checks that the subtitles explain the visual content before saving the anchor. To compare agents with people, we introduce LEAF-Bench, which pairs 502 lecture videos spanning 81.9 hours and eleven subjects with 4,779 human reference anchors and 41,294 human browsing actions collected through the same player interface. Across fifteen VLM backbones evaluated on LEAF-Bench, anchor recall ranges from only 42.1% to 77.1%, despite observation rates of 97.6% to 99.5% in our trajectory analysis. Most missed anchors are discarded after observation, either during content evaluation or when verifying alignment between visuals and narration. Agents also produce shorter and more fragmented anchors than people. Within model families, larger backbones generally narrow these gaps. These findings identify instructional-value judgment, visual-narration alignment, and the preservation of complete explanatory units as key directions for advancing GUI agents in visual knowledge anchor collection.

Method

Skim. Pause. Rewind. Keep.

LEAF is an agent harness of five modules. The kernel sees and acts on the player, procedural skills skim, rewind and record, the behavioral state machine decides what runs next, autonomic fallbacks recover from failures, and the executive planner asks one vision–language model four kinds of questions.
