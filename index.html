Abstract

Educational videos teach through what they show — a radiograph, a specimen, a map, a painting — explained as the video unfolds. A person studying such a video pauses when something is shown, rewinds to where its explanation began, and keeps the few moments worth returning to. We ask whether multimodal agents can do the same. We define active, evidence-grounded visual knowledge collection: an agent operates a real video player and collects knowledge moments — intervals of a video, each kept with a clean frame of what it shows and the subtitles that explain it. Our agent goes from a topic to such moments: it searches, chooses videos, and on each video pauses, rewinds, and keeps, with one vision–language model making every judgment about content. We build a benchmark of 502 videos from 11 subjects in which annotators kept the moments they found worth keeping, and compare eight open backbones against these references. The best agent keeps a frame inside 77% of the moments people kept, the weakest 42%; agents had seen almost all the moments they missed and lost them afterwards, in judging, verifying, or picking the frame.

Method

Skim. Pause. Rewind. Keep.

One vision–language model judges the content; deterministic tools do the browsing. Every step runs on the live player — no downloaded video, no transcript API. Scroll to follow one moment the agent kept.

  1. 01

    Skim.

    Sweep the timeline in growing jumps while nothing on screen changes.

    Rule-based seeks · each new frame compared with the last
  2. 02

    Pause.

    A new visual appears. The model looks at the frame before and after, and decides it is worth a closer look.

    Visual-change triage · two frames
  3. 03

    Rewind.

    Bisect back to where the visual begins, then record until it changes again.

    Bisection to a four-second window · then recording
  4. 04

    Keep.

    Choose the frame that shows it best, and keep the moment only if its subtitles explain it.

Human annotation

A labeler that records how people watch

Annotators use the same player actions as the agent. Every skim, look-back and inspection is logged, so the benchmark captures both what people select and how they found it.
